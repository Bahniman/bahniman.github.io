    Case study 01 · Product analytics

    Low adoption that pointed to a findability problem

    Problem
    Pendo was mostly used for surveys, so adoption after a release was hard to judge and roadmap talks leaned on escalations.
    What I did
    Tagged 700+ features and built funnel and path views over imports, lifecycle changes and settings.
    The call
    People entered nearby flows but never reached the features. That is findability, not demand, so plain names, help text and placement came before any rebuild.
    Result
    Clearer names, help text and a reorganised settings area shipped, and the approach shaped the wider settings audit.

    Case study 02 · Localisation

    Making localisation work in context

    Problem
    Strings were translated one by one and hard-coded; native-speaking clients said some literal translations did not fit where they appeared.
    Foundation
    An earlier effort had centralised translations. I built on it with a workflow for client-facing teams: navigation, access, ownership, and one way to update strings across PHP and Angular.
    The call
    I mapped recurring issues across settings, raised the workflow for the roadmap, and owned the PRD end to end once it was prioritised.
    Result
    Turnaround went from waiting for a sprint to the same release cycle, across 10+ languages.

    Case study 03 · Strategy · XLRI

    An IPO-readiness deck where every number had to survive a question

    Problem
    Was Aster DM Healthcare's Gulf business ready for an IPO? The argument leaned on peer comparisons and penalty figures, many from secondary sources.
    What I did
    Traced every figure to a primary or named source. Anything untraceable was removed, not softened.
    The call
    Two peers' share-price declines covered different periods, so I found the offer price and restated both on the same basis.
    Result
    Eight corrections before the panel, and a top 3 finish out of 20 teams.

    Windtunnel

    Plays a price increase out 500 times across three customer groups and shows who leaves. A flat 12% rise holds up; at 30% it loses money in more than half the runs.

    Ask me · Career

    Why move from civil engineering to software?

    Darwinbox came to campus early and I applied out of curiosity. Product work showed me the habit of planning a build in detail carries over. A PRD does for a software team what a good drawing does for a site.

    Ask me · Career

    Why an MBA after 45 months?

    To build on the product work with a broader business view. Working with my manager and director showed me how decisions bring client needs, team priorities and business goals together. Both are XLRI alumni and backed my application.

    Ask me · Working style

    How do you keep engineers unblocked?

    Edge cases, acceptance criteria and dependencies in every PRD, then a knowledge transfer with the developers before the sprint. The document carries the shared understanding; follow-ups are only for decisions that need one.

    Ask me · Judgement

    What if your own feature doesn't land?

    My features reached 5% to 60% account-level adoption, and one I had high hopes for sat at the low end. I suspected rollout, then the drop-off data made me reconsider the design itself. I'd rather test assumptions in planning than let clients find the limits.
