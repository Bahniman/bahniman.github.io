Darwinbox

Enterprise HRMS, serving clients across APAC, MENA, Europe and North America. July 2022 to March 2026. Product Analyst, then Senior Product Analyst from April 2025. Forty five months, one promotion, three junior analysts mentored, and an Exemplary Contributor award.

The document is the job

I wrote the spec engineering built from, across the employee lifecycle, org structure, access controls and bulk data flows. The ones that worked carried the edge cases and the test notes, so questions got answered in the document instead of in a call three weeks later. I tracked which developer would pick up which spec in the coming sprint and ran the handover before anyone was blocked. Across 45 months that came to more than 200 engineering-ready documents. Features released this way landed between 5 and 60 percent account-level adoption in the quarters after launch, and I benchmarked what we were building against the top five HRMS platforms on the Gartner Magic Quadrant, so parity was a design input rather than a gap someone found later.

I built the company’s eyesight

Pendo was already paid for when I joined, but two or three people used it and only to float in-app surveys. Nobody used the analytics. I dug into it out of curiosity and found it could show what people actually did: click paths, funnel drop-offs, how often a feature got touched. So I built the usage-analytics function from nothing. Several genuinely good features turned out to be barely used. They were buried, or the setting had a name that meant nothing to the person reading it. Nobody reads release notes, so the product has to explain itself. I rewrote configuration names in plain language, added help text, and moved settings to where people would actually look, then used Pendo to prove usage climbed. I contributed to the dashboard revamp that reached over 322,000 enterprise users, where time spent on key actions fell 45 percent.

Lost in literal translation

Every new string was hard-coded and translated by pasting it into ChatGPT, by developers who did not speak the language. Clients who did speak it kept flagging translations that were technically correct and meaningless in context. The company brought in a tech architect and shipped a central page for editing translations, which leadership read as done. It was not. The URL was per-client and hidden with no clear owner, strings sat in different files per module across a mixed PHP and Angular codebase, and finding the broken one was painful. I went looking myself, wrote up what was actually wrong, and it became a roadmap item I owned the PRD for. The product now runs across more than 50 languages without engineering in the loop. Being in product is what let me fix the system instead of one string at a time.

Make the import survive first contact

Bulk import is where enterprise HR data actually enters the system, and it was where admins failed most often. I redesigned it around validation, format handling and field-level guidance so the first attempt succeeded and neither support nor the client had to clean up afterwards. I also specified a conversational import assistant, where an admin describes the intent in plain language and the system generates the file, catches the errors and routes approvals. It was specified in full and never built.

The trust layer for the agent economy

Four open-source protocols, built in order. Software is starting to transact on our behalf, and each one covers a step that has to hold up.

01

Realium, Settlement

A settlement rail for public works. It turns site-verified construction progress into a receivable a bank will actually lend against, so contractors stop waiting out the payment cycle. Sixty percent of the invoice reaches the contractor on day one instead of day 148, against an Ed25519 attestation signed on site. Placed top 3 at the ReEnvision 5.0 conclave, judged by senior technology leaders. See the demo or the source.

02

Heirloom, Memory

Institutional memory that stays with the firm. Queries are scoped by role-based cryptography and the schema is portable and model independent, so the context survives the partner who leaves and the vendor you switch away from. See the demo or the source.

03

Turnstile, Commerce

Commerce analytics for the agent era. It tells human sessions apart from AI agents. That lets it recover checkouts bot defences decline by mistake, and serve agents a machine-readable storefront instead of a page they have to guess at. See the demo or the source.

04

Windtunnel, Simulation

A way to test a pricing change before customers feel it. It builds synthetic cohorts from real support and transaction history, then runs the change thousands of times, so you find where retention breaks before anyone real is affected. See the demo or the source.

The Codex

FULCRUM 2026, legacy systems and AI, with Nandini

Modernisation is sold on reading old code, and that capability has commoditised: the leading specialist tool beats a general-purpose model by one point on the industry benchmark, and loses to it on the hardest tier. I audited every claim in our draft, found two that did not survive checking, and rebuilt the argument around what the benchmark actually showed. The failure is that the reasoning behind each rule was never recorded, so every change re-derives it by hand and the migration converts a specification nobody verified. Read the case.
